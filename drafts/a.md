LEAD

An Optimizely breach turned 178 organizations' websites into crypto address swappers.

Attackers wrote malicious JavaScript into Optimizely customer projects on 16 November 2026, and the vendor's own CDN served it to visitors of sites including Ledger, HSBC and the BBC. The wallets show a small haul, but stolen cookies, card data and iOS exploit attempts leave no trace on a chain.

The first injection appeared at 04:21 UTC on 16 November 2026, and most sites stayed exposed for about 16 hours [https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer]. Scott Helme of Report URI found the code in 188 of the 1,611 Optimizely snippets he checked, belonging to 178 organizations [https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer]. Named sites include CNN, Zoom, Salesforce, GitLab, HSBC, IKEA and the hardware-wallet maker Ledger [https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer]. Six of the affected organizations are crypto and trading firms, against 32 in banking and finance [https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer].

The payload replaced ETH, BTC, TRX, TON, SOL, LTC, BCH and DOGE addresses in page text, form fields and the clipboard [https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer]. It also carried a card skimmer and loaded iOS exploit pages that Helme links to the Coruna and DarkSword kits [https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer]. Public scans show those pages being delivered, not exploits succeeding [https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer].

On-chain, the BTC address 19g9rZcPeiz8rUvyWpTRyXXhPnjsWrc3kW received 0.0853 BTC and was emptied at 05:39 UTC on 17 November 2026 [https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer]. The ETH address 0xC4026b06D82b0F0B363eda0642D61CfbDc17884e received 0.0745 ETH and the TRON address took 662.9 USDT [https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer]. Estimates of the take range from about $2,300 sent by victims, per Galaxy's Alex Thorn, to Helme's $7,040 [https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer]. Decrypt puts it near $7,150 [https://decrypt.co/383414/optimizely-hack-hijacked-ledger-bbc-and-176-other-sites-to-swap-crypto-wallet-addresses].

Optimizely said it "identified and contained a security incident" and that "the injected code was removed" [https://decrypt.co/383414/optimizely-hack-hijacked-ledger-bbc-and-176-other-sites-to-swap-crypto-wallet-addresses]. A spokesperson told Gizmodo the flaw sat in the account signup flow, as relayed by Decrypt [https://decrypt.co/383414/optimizely-hack-hijacked-ledger-bbc-and-176-other-sites-to-swap-crypto-wallet-addresses]. The company has not explained how one signup reached unrelated customers' projects [https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer]. Anyone who sent crypto from an affected site on 16 November 2026 should check the destination address against their wallet screen [https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer].

NEWS

[NEWS] An attacker took $38,200 of Curve DAO fee revenue through its CoW Protocol burner.
Curve's disclosure says the theft happened on 10 November 2026 and that no user funds, pools or crvUSD markets were involved [https://github.com/curvefi/security-incident-reports/blob/main/disclosures/2026-11-10.md]. The CowSwapBurner approved orders without checking that the order it inspected matched the hash being executed, so anyone could sell its balances to any receiver [https://github.com/curvefi/security-incident-reports/blob/main/disclosures/2026-11-10.md]. The loss is about two-fifths of the 97,317.74 crvUSD that the same week's regular conversion delivered to the DAO [https://github.com/curvefi/security-incident-reports/blob/main/disclosures/2026-11-10.md]. The Emergency DAO disabled the burner at 13:59 UTC that day, and DAO vote 1524 proposes a replacement [https://github.com/curvefi/security-incident-reports/blob/main/disclosures/2026-11-10.md].

[NEWS] Four Democratic senators pressed the SEC on its tokenized-stock exemption.
Senators Angela Alsobrooks, Ruben Gallego, Mark Warner and Catherine Cortez Masto wrote to SEC Chair Paul Atkins, The Block reported on 16 November 2026 [https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-11-16-democratic-senators-warn-secs-tokenized-stock-exemption-illicit-finance-gaps-441432]. They warned a tokenized share bought by a verified buyer could end up "beyond the reach of the U.S. regulatory perimeter" [https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-11-16-democratic-senators-warn-secs-tokenized-stock-exemption-illicit-finance-gaps-441432]. The letter concedes that tokenization "may well improve efficiencies in the market and broaden access" [https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-11-16-democratic-senators-warn-secs-tokenized-stock-exemption-illicit-finance-gaps-441432]. The Bitcoin Foundation's news site counts 13 questions with a 30-day deadline [https://bitcoinfoundation.org/news/regulation/four-senators-question-sec-tokenized-stock-exemption-over-illicit-finance-gaps/].

[NEWS] The SEC filed 348 enforcement actions in fiscal 2026 and named crypto fraud among its examples.
The total includes 235 standalone actions and 113 follow-on proceedings that bar or suspend individuals [https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-113-sec-announces-enforcement-annual-results-fiscal-year-2026]. The agency's 10 November 2026 release cites charges against three purported crypto trading platforms and four investment clubs over at least $14 million taken from retail investors [https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-113-sec-announces-enforcement-annual-results-fiscal-year-2026]. TECH

[TECH] Simulations show the post-quantum mempool's upload cost turns on how many peers receive each proof.
Csaba Kiraly published network simulations of EIP-8288, the recursive-STARK mempool by Vitalik Buterin and Thomas Coratger, on 18 November 2026 [https://ethresear.ch/t/eip-8288-the-networking-side-of-recursive-aggregation/26263]. At 15 signature transactions per second, the current mempool design carried post-quantum signatures at about 0.4 to 1 Mbit/s of upload per node [https://ethresear.ch/t/eip-8288-the-networking-side-of-recursive-aggregation/26263]. Pushing proofs to six peers every interval cost 11.9 to 13.3 Mbit/s, about half of each node's modelled 25 Mbit/s uplink [https://ethresear.ch/t/eip-8288-the-networking-side-of-recursive-aggregation/26263]. Letting peers fetch proofs on demand cut that to 2.4 Mbit/s at 2 transactions per second, and the post asks EIP-8288 to specify a small push fan-out [https://ethresear.ch/t/eip-8288-the-networking-side-of-recursive-aggregation/26263].

[TECH] A fee-market paper finds EIP-7999 delivers more execution than its one-dimensional rivals.
Fei Wu, writing from an Ethereum Foundation internship mentored by Maria Silva, compared EIP-7999's multidimensional fees with tuned single-price designs [https://ethresear.ch/t/more-meters-or-more-prices-one-vs-multi-dimensional-fee-mechanisms-on-ethereum/26244]. Raising the single-price design's target ratio from 1/2 to 4/5 lifts execution to 199.0M to 215.0M gas per block, but near-limit blocks rise from about 4% to as much as 20.6% [https://ethresear.ch/t/more-meters-or-more-prices-one-vs-multi-dimensional-fee-mechanisms-on-ethereum/26244]. Conservative EIP-7999 settings deliver 4% to 34% more execution at a similar congestion share, with state growth at its 120 GiB per year target [https://ethresear.ch/t/more-meters-or-more-prices-one-vs-multi-dimensional-fee-mechanisms-on-ethereum/26244]. These are simulation results, and the author published code to reproduce them [https://ethresear.ch/t/more-meters-or-more-prices-one-vs-multi-dimensional-fee-mechanisms-on-ethereum/26244].
