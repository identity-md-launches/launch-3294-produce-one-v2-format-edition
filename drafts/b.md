LEAD

A federal judge acquitted the Peraire-Bueno brothers of the $25 million MEV-Boost fraud charges.

The ruling ends a federal case that treated exploiting Ethereum's block-building software as wire fraud. The judge held that the government never proved a material lie.

U.S. District Judge Jessica Clarke entered a judgment of acquittal for Anton and James Peraire-Bueno, two former MIT students [https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.621224/gov.uscourts.nysd.621224.294.0.pdf]. Reuters reported the decision on 16 November 2026 [https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-judge-clears-mit-educated-brothers-25-million-cryptocurrency-heist-charges-2026-11-16/]. Clarke wrote that "the Government failed to prove that the Peraire-Buenos made any material misrepresentations," according to BleepingComputer's account of the order [https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/court-clears-brothers-of-25-million-ethereum-crypto-heist-charges/].

The brothers faced wire fraud and money laundering counts, each carrying up to 20 years [https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/court-clears-brothers-of-25-million-ethereum-crypto-heist-charges/]. Prosecutors alleged they used bait transactions to lure trading bots, exploited a flaw in MEV-Boost relay code, and reordered a proposed block [https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1351996/dl]. The indictment says the scheme took about $25 million from those traders in roughly 12 seconds [https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1351996/dl]. They were arrested in May 2024 [https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/court-clears-brothers-of-25-million-ethereum-crypto-heist-charges/].

The acquittal follows a mistrial about a year earlier, when jurors could not agree on a verdict [https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/court-clears-brothers-of-25-million-ethereum-crypto-heist-charges/]. No response from prosecutors had been reported as of this edition [https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/court-clears-brothers-of-25-million-ethereum-crypto-heist-charges/].

The stakes reach past two defendants. Prosecutors argued that gaming a protocol's rules could be fraud; the judge found no misrepresentation to anyone [https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.621224/gov.uscourts.nysd.621224.294.0.pdf]. That is the paper's inference about the order's significance, not a holding on code-is-law questions, and an appeal or a different fact pattern could move the line. Searchers, builders and relay operators now have one federal ruling to point to. They do not yet have a rule.

NEWS

[NEWS] Attackers used Optimizely's platform to plant a crypto address swapper on 178 organizations' websites.
On 16 November 2026 malicious JavaScript was written into the Optimizely Web projects of 178 organizations, including Ledger, the BBC, CNN, HSBC and Zoom, according to StepSecurity's reconstruction [https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer]. The code replaced checksum-valid wallet addresses with the attacker's, including ETH address 0xC4026b06D82b0F0B363eda0642D61CfbDc17884e, and also carried a card skimmer and iPhone exploit loaders [https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer]. Estimates of the take are small and disputed: Decrypt cites Report URI at about $7,150, while StepSecurity cites a Galaxy Digital estimate near $2,300 that it could not confirm [https://decrypt.co/383414/optimizely-hack-hijacked-ledger-bbc-and-176-other-sites-to-swap-crypto-wallet-addresses] [https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer]. Optimizely says an unauthorized party exploited a vulnerability in its Experimentation product, which it has fixed [https://decrypt.co/383414/optimizely-hack-hijacked-ledger-bbc-and-176-other-sites-to-swap-crypto-wallet-addresses].

[NEWS] A former Robinhood engineer pleaded guilty to trading Hyperliquid perpetuals on secret listing plans.
Huaisong Xiang pleaded guilty to one count of wire fraud in the Southern District of New York, The Block reported on 17 November 2026 [https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-11-17-former-robinhood-engineer-pleads-guilty-to-wire-fraud-over-trades-ahead-of-crypto-listing-announcements-442106]. He used confidential information about planned Robinhood Crypto listings to trade perpetual futures on Hyperliquid and agreed to forfeit $125,523 in proceeds, The Block reported, citing the U.S. Attorney's Office [https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-11-17-former-robinhood-engineer-pleads-guilty-to-wire-fraud-over-trades-ahead-of-crypto-listing-announcements-442106] [https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/former-robinhood-employee-pleads-guilty-fraud]. Sentencing is set for 25 February 2027; the count carries a maximum of 20 years [https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-11-17-former-robinhood-engineer-pleads-guilty-to-wire-fraud-over-trades-ahead-of-crypto-listing-announcements-442106].

[NEWS] Kenya's regulators told every crypto platform serving Kenyans to get licensed or face fines up to KES 25 million.
The Central Bank of Kenya and the Capital Markets Authority issued a joint notice on 13 November 2026 operationalizing the 2026 virtual asset regulations [https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/CBK-CMA-Public-Notice-Operationalisation-of-the-VASP-Regulations-2026-.pdf]. The law's one-year compliance window closed on 4 November 2026, and companies face fines of up to KES 25 million on conviction, according to Tech-ish [https://tech-ish.com/2026/11/17/crypto-firms-kenya-cbk-cma-licence-fines] [https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2025/20/eng@2025-11-04]. Minimum paid-up capital runs from KES 100 million for an exchange to KES 300 million for a stablecoin issuer, per Tech-ish's reading of the regulations [https://tech-ish.com/2026/11/17/crypto-firms-kenya-cbk-cma-licence-fines] [https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/2026/134/eng@2026-07-22]. Neither regulator had published a list of licensed providers as of 17 November 2026 [https://tech-ish.com/2026/11/17/crypto-firms-kenya-cbk-cma-licence-fines].

TECH

[TECH] Glamsterdam's gas repricing could break up to 15,000 Ethereum contracts.
Marius van der Wijden of the Ethereum Foundation told the 16 November 2026 core developer call that EIP-8037 and EIP-8038 could disrupt up to 15,000 contracts, out of roughly 90 million, CoinDesk reported [https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/11/17/ethereum-s-glamsterdam-upgrade-risks-breaking-up-to-15-000-contracts]. EIP-8037 would raise the cost of deploying a contract to about 183,600 gas from 32,000 and of a new storage slot to about 97,920 gas from 20,000 [https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/11/17/ethereum-s-glamsterdam-upgrade-risks-breaking-up-to-15-000-contracts]. Audits by ChainSecurity and Dedaub plus Foundation research produced roughly 200 combined findings, and the call ended without agreement [https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/11/17/ethereum-s-glamsterdam-upgrade-risks-breaking-up-to-15-000-contracts]. Decrypt reports some developers warned that dropping the changes could delay the upgrade by four to six months [https://decrypt.co/383462/ethereum-devs-scaling-upgrade-could-break-15000-apps].

[TECH] Csaba Kiraly published simulations of how a proving mempool for post-quantum signatures would behave.
On 18 November 2026 Kiraly posted results on the networking side of EIP-8288, the recursive-STARK mempool specified by Vitalik Buterin and Thomas Coratger [https://ethresear.ch/t/eip-8288-the-networking-side-of-recursive-aggregation/26263]. He reports that Ethereum's current mempool design can carry post-quantum signatures at about 15 signature transactions per second, with nodes uploading about 0.4 Mbit/s for 2.5 kB signatures [https://ethresear.ch/t/eip-8288-the-networking-side-of-recursive-aggregation/26263]. His simulator varied who aggregates and when, across networks of 100 to 10,000 nodes [https://ethresear.ch/t/eip-8288-the-networking-side-of-recursive-aggregation/26263]. The post argues most results turn on choices EIP-8288 leaves open, and names a parallel wire-protocol effort, EIP-8437 [https://ethresear.ch/t/eip-8288-the-networking-side-of-recursive-aggregation/26263].

[CULTURE] Molly White argues the crypto lobby lost the midterms it claims to have won.
In a 13 November 2026 post, White writes that crypto and AI super PACs spent $250 million on the 2026 elections [https://www.citationneeded.news/crypto-ai-lost-the-midterms/]. She counts Fairshake at 74 for 74 where its candidate was favored but 10 for 18 in competitive races, with about half of its $154.5 million behind losers [https://www.citationneeded.news/crypto-ai-lost-the-midterms/]. Her headline example is Ohio, where the industry spent $64 million against Sherrod Brown and he won [https://www.citationneeded.news/crypto-ai-lost-the-midterms/]. She sets this against the "roughly 92% success rate" a Fairshake spokesman supplied to Crypto In America [https://www.citationneeded.news/crypto-ai-lost-the-midterms/].

[CULTURE] ENS token holders are voting on whether to hand endowment policy to the Foundation board.
The amendment would move oversight of the ENS Endowment's Investment Policy Statement from the Meta-Governance Working Group to the ENS Foundation board, TokenPost reported on 16 November 2026 [https://www.tokenpost.com/news/business/59048]. The board could then set allocations and hire or fire the endowment manager without a DAO vote each time, while the DAO keeps a power to reverse amendments [https://www.tokenpost.com/news/business/59048]. TokenPost gives 21 November 2026 as the close; the step follows an August 2026 vote that gave the Foundation administrative control of a $65 million endowment [https://www.tokenpost.com/news/business/59048] [https://theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2026-08-11-ens-foundation-administrative-control-65-million-endowment-tokenholder-vote-411464]. Whether DAOs should delegate to small boards is the open question; this vote is one answer in progress.
